## Fix

**Rung 5: a client drag path.** `TerminalClickFocusMonitor`'s `leftMouseDown` handling calls `window.performDrag(with:)` and consumes the press when `TitleStripDrag.shouldStartDrag` says so:

- the window is non-opaque,
- `clickCount == 1`,
- the point is in the title strip (`locationInWindow.y >= window.contentLayoutRect.maxY`),
- neither Control nor Command is held,
- and the hit view's exact class name is on the allowlist: `NSToolbarPrimaryTitleContainerView`, `NSToolbarTitleView`, `_NSToolbarTitleField`, `NSTitlebarView`.

**Why those classes.** The first three are what the user's log shows for the presses that failed. `NSTitlebarView` wasn't in that log; it comes from the rung and the replica's hit test. `NSToolbarView` is left out, because no hit test here or in the log ever returned it. A renamed AppKit class falls back to today's behaviour.

**Opaque windows take today's path.** The first guard is `!window.isOpaque`, so at 100 there's no extra hit test, no drag call and no change. Probes and the probe log are reverted (`#0548 Revert the probes`). Fix A and removing the `ignoresMouseEvents` write aren't applied, because the user's test showed neither helps.

**The title field stays on the list.** `_NSToolbarTitleField` has no function in Batty: `navigationTitle` takes a plain `String` in `SessionDetailView` and a `Text` in `SessionSidebarView`, and a grep of `Batty` and `BattyKit/Sources` finds no `toolbarTitleMenu`, `navigationDocument`, `representedURL` or `representedFilename`. Session rename doesn't go through it.

**State.** The press is consumed with `withholdingClick` cleared before the drag starts, so no later drag or mouse-up event can be swallowed, whether or not the mouse-up reaches the monitor. No model is written and there's no `Task`.

**Additions beyond the rung.** Both are there because a consumed press never reaches AppKit:

- A press on a window that isn't key raises it, and an inactive app activates, before the drag. These are the responder changes the observation rules permit from an event monitor. Neither is measured.
- A Control or Command press passes through, so a Control-click stays a context-menu click and a Command-drag keeps whatever AppKit does with it. In the band that may still mean no drag, the same as today.

## Gotchas

- **A pass in the guest isn't proof for a real display or for macOS 27.** These tests can't confirm a fix for this bug by themselves: they pass with and without one. They guard against the chrome breaking in the guest.
- XCUITest's press and drag are synthesized, which is one more way the guest's path may differ from a real pointer.
- The `before-gesture` screenshots show black Sidebar and title text on Dracula in a light-mode guest. It wasn't investigated here and has nothing to do with dragging.
- A window drag that the server handles may never deliver a mouse-down to the app, so a missing press line in the probe log means something only when the window also stayed put.
- **The fix treats the symptom.** The cause is unknown, and `performDrag` goes around it. If a later macOS changes the title views' class names, the band goes back to not dragging, and nothing else breaks.
- **The new title-height tests are an estimate.** 26 points from the top is a guess at the title's vertical centre, since the log has no window height. They can't prove the fix either, because the guest passed without one. They do exercise the client path if the press reaches the app there, including the double-click after a consumed first press.
- **Things only the user's Mac can confirm, at 80 and 0, with and without a Theme:**
  1. Dragging across the whole title strip over the terminal moves the window, including from the session title's text.
  2. Double-click on the band zooms. The first press is consumed and starts a drag, the second has `clickCount == 2` and goes to AppKit. Whether AppKit still zooms from it isn't verified.
  3. A single click on the band with no movement does nothing odd, and the next click in a terminal works.
  4. Pressing the band of a background Batty window, and of Batty while another app is frontmost, raises it and drags it.
  5. **Resizing from the top edge and the top corners over the terminal still works.** Review's replica found no overlap between the resize band and the allowlist (`## Verification`), but no real window has been resized on this fix. A resize that turns into a drag would be a regression, whichever class it hit.
  6. Traffic lights, toolbar buttons and the Sidebar side behave as at 100.
  7. **With an alert up (Quit confirmation, for one), pressing the title strip of a see-through window behind it.** The fix calls `makeKeyAndOrderFront` on a window that isn't key and has no modal check, so the window behind might take key status from the alert. Return and Esc should still answer the alert. Not measured. Same question for a window with a sheet attached (the Project editor's open panel).

## Plan

Written 2026-11-03 against `719b32a3`. "Measured" means a throwaway Swift process on this Mac (macOS 27.0), now deleted. It had a real SwiftUI `WindowGroup` with `NavigationSplitView`, `.toolbar`, `.navigationTitle`, a host with this `hitTest`, a tab bar and a divider with these `.background`s, and the same band. ### What was measured

**AppKit's in-process answer is the same at 100 and at 80, with and without a Theme.** So hypotheses 1, 4 and 5 don't hold as written, for this replica.

| Measured at a title bar point | Opaque | See-through | With a Theme |
|---|---|---|---|
| `hitTest` over the detail column | `NSToolbarTitleView` < `NSToolbarView` < `NSTitlebarView` | The same | The same |
| `mouseDownCanMoveWindow` along that chain | True on every view | The same | The same |
| `NSThemeFrame.shouldStartWindowDragForEvent:`, private | True in the title bar, false in the body | The same | The same |
| `window._lastDragRegionDataDescription`, private, the region AppKit gives the server | 79 rectangles, holes only at the traffic lights, toolbar platters and split | Byte-identical apart from the pointer | Byte-identical across both states |
| `TerminalHostView`-like host's frame | Below the title bar, height ends at the strip | The same | The same |
| Pixel alpha, title bar over the detail column and over the Sidebar | 1.0 and 1.0 | 1.0 and 1.0 | 1.0 and 1.0 |
| Pixel alpha, detail body | 1.0 | 0.004 | 0.004 |

The pixels were also solid with key and active appearance forced by swizzling, with the tab bar's `chromeBacking` and the divider's in place. **Pixel alpha is an offscreen `CGWindowListCreateImage` of an unlaunched replica, not a real display.**

**The full-window `NSVisualEffectView` isn't the answer.** It sits below the hit path, and an opaque window with a Theme has no such view either (it isn't in the tree once `backgroundColor` is set), and those windows are what Prod ships.

**The server sees one new tag from this change.** `SLSGetWindowTags`, private:

| Writes | Tags |
|---|---|
| Opaque, fresh | `0x482001` |
| `isOpaque = false` and the 0.004 color, no `ignoresMouseEvents` write, after a run loop turn | `0x482001` |
| The same plus `ignoresMouseEvents = false` | **`0x482401`** |
| Flip back to opaque, then non-opaque again | `0x482401`, it stays |

AppKit's `setIgnoresMouseEvents:` calls `_NSSetIgnoresMouseEvents`, which calls `_NSSetWindowTags`, read from its disassembly. So bit `0x400` is the server-side effect of the explicit write. **The bit's meaning is an inference:** presumably "take events regardless of pixel alpha". It stays set at 100 after a flip, and the user reports dragging at 100, presumably after a flip. So the tag alone doesn't break dragging; at most it does so together with non-opaque.

**The one positional difference found: what AppKit does after the press.** A `leftMouseDown` passed to `sendEvent`:

| Point | No Theme (system title bar) | Theme (`titlebarAppearsTransparent`) |
|---|---|---|
| Title bar over the Sidebar | No client drag. Hit `NSTitlebarView` | `performWindowDragWithEvent:` from `_handleLeftMouseDownEvent:` |
| Title bar over the detail column | No client drag. Hit `NSToolbarTitleView` | `performWindowDragWithEvent:` |
| Terminal body, tab bar | None | None |

This is the same in both opacity states. **With no Theme, AppKit leaves the drag entirely to the window server** (`_lastDragRegionData`, `_updateWindowCanServerSideDrag`), so nothing in the app can rescue a server that declines. With a Theme, AppKit starts the drag itself from the mouse-down. `_shouldStartWindowDragForEvent:` on the window reads true only with the transparent title bar. The window was inactive in these runs, so some of this may differ when key.

**Upstream Ghostty:** `TerminalWindow.swift:487-493` sets `isOpaque = false` and white at 0.001, and `ignoresMouseEvents` appears nowhere in `macos/Sources`. The standard terminal window has no drag code: `performDrag` is only in the titlebar-tabs style's `WindowDragView`, `mouseDownCanMoveWindow` only in `NonDraggableHostingView` on an update accessory, and `isMovableByWindowBackground` only in `AboutController`. It has no `NavigationSplitView`. The two Batty-only differences are the explicit `ignoresMouseEvents = false` and the 1/255 window color.

### Most likely cause

**The window server declines the drag over the detail column in a non-opaque window, and with no Theme AppKit has no client path to fall back on.** Confidence: **medium** that the failure is on the server side of that split rather than in AppKit's views, **low** on why. Rough odds on what makes the server decline: tag `0x400` together with non-opaque 35%, something about pixels on a real display that the offscreen sample can't show 35%, something only the real app has that the replica doesn't 30%. The Sidebar still working isn't explained by any of the tags; a Sidebar-only client path or the pixels under it are the remaining candidates.

**Prediction this makes for the VM:** with no Theme the first test fails, with a Theme it passes. If the user had a Theme active, this model is wrong, which is why step 1 tests both.

**Fix A: send a see-through window down the same path a themed window already uses.** In `WindowSeeThrough.apply`'s see-through branch, `window.titlebarAppearsTransparent = true` unconditionally. In `WindowSeeThrough.titlebarFill`, `isSeeThrough ? solid(windowBackground) : .clear`, so the existing zero-height band in `SessionDetailView` paints the title bar. Measured with this change on a no-Theme see-through replica: `performWindowDragWithEvent:` fires over the Sidebar and the detail column, not in the terminal body or the tab bar, and title bar pixels stay at alpha 1 everywhere.

Why this one:
- It adds no event handling and no `performDrag` of our own. AppKit's own path decides, the same one opaque themed windows have in Prod today, so the toolbar and traffic lights behave as they do there.
- A press in a terminal never reaches it, so selection is untouched. No `isMovableByWindowBackground`.
- It stays in the applier's deferred hop, with no `Task` and no model write. The opaque branch doesn't change by a character, so 100 and Prod defaults make exactly today's writes.
- Below 100 the desktop still shows through: only the strip the band covers is painted, the same as with a Theme today.

Cost: with no Theme, the see-through title bar's color changes from the system's (0.15 gray, dark) to `.windowBackgroundColor` (0.12, dark), which matches the see-through Sidebar's fill. The user's call to accept, on the checklist. The hook also fired at toolbar buttons and the close button while the window was inactive, both here and on today's themed opaque window, so the same risk exists in Prod; `testToolbarButtonWorksBelowFullOpacity` and the minimize test cover it.

Fix A's cost if the press never reaches the app: it can't help. Then rung 1 onward.

## Hypotheses

**All unproven. Each needs a measurement before a fix is chosen.** The Plan should start by telling them apart, not by picking one.

1. **AppKit's title bar routing changes when the window goes non-opaque.** Clue, measured in #0539's plan: while opaque, `NSThemeFrame` holds a full-window `NSVisualEffectView`, and AppKit removes it when `isOpaque` goes false. Something that view provided in the hit path, such as answering `mouseDownCanMoveWindow`, may go with it. It fits "works at 100". What's missing is why the Sidebar side would still work. Its new solid fill might be the answer, or the Sidebar's own hosting view may answer differently.
2. **The window server passes the press on, or treats the region differently, because it is nearly transparent.** The window color is 1/255 alpha, and #0539's offscreen check of click-through reported a miss everywhere, so it said nothing. VM run `20261103-183428-60135` passed `TerminalSeeThroughTests` (#0544), so a click at opacity 0 reached a Pane in the guest. That counts against this one for a terminal rectangle in the VM, but it doesn't cover the title bar or a real display. #0539's probe measured the title bar over the detail column at alpha 1, offscreen and with no real desktop, and that counts against this one, at least with no Theme. The Sidebar has solid paint under its part of the title bar and the detail column doesn't, which fits. If the press goes to the app behind, that app would activate or take the click.
3. **`ignoresMouseEvents = false`, set explicitly, changes how the window takes events.** From memory, the explicit value makes a non-opaque window take events it would otherwise pass on. That should help, not harm, but it is new on this path and trivially removable in a probe.
4. **The clear band removed whatever made the region draggable.** This can hold **only with a Theme**, because with no Theme the band is `.clear` at 100 too and dragging works there. A SwiftUI color view may answer `mouseDownCanMoveWindow` differently from nothing at all (from memory, `NSView`'s default depends on whether the view is opaque; check it).
5. **The terminal host or a clear SwiftUI layer sits under the title bar and takes the press.** Weakest on reading: its frame and `hitTest` don't change with opacity, so it would have to fail at 100 as well. What could still change is a view's answer to `mouseDownCanMoveWindow` once the window or backdrop isn't opaque, which merges into 1 or 4. The host's real frame relative to the toolbar safe area wasn't measured.

**Theme dependence is unknown.** The report doesn't say whether a Theme was active.

### Cheap ways to tell them apart

- **Ask the user two things:** was a Theme active, and does the app behind react to the press (activate, take the click)? A reaction points at 2, and no reaction points at routing inside AppKit (1, 3, 4, 5).
- **No Session selected, while see-through.** `backdropFill` paints solid there. If the title bar drags, paint or the band matters (2 or 4).
- **In-process hit test, no synthetic input.** In a throwaway process, or a temporary log in a Beta build, walk `window.contentView?.superview?.hitTest(point)` at a title bar point over the detail column and over the Sidebar, then the view's `mouseDownCanMoveWindow` and its superviews', opaque versus non-opaque, with and without a Theme. A difference there settles 1, 4 and 5 without touching the window server. This doesn't need a shown window, unlike #0539's click-through check.
- **Remove one change at a time** in a probe: `ignoresMouseEvents`, the band, the alpha of the window color (1/255 versus something visibly higher).
- **Log `sendEvent`** in the VM to see whether a mouse-down reaches the window at all.

Never script input on the host (`AGENTS.md`, "UI test automation"). Anything synthetic goes in the VM.
