## Description

After the #0127 re-resolution (dedicated drag handle, `PaneSwapDropTarget` on terminal body), the drop zone highlight is unreliable. Dragging a pane handle over another pane often does not highlight that pane at all; the highlight only appears when the cursor is near the edge of the target pane, not anywhere inside it.

## Expected behavior

Dragging the grip handle over any part of another pane highlights the entire pane immediately and consistently, regardless of where over the target the cursor is.

## Actual behavior

The drop zone highlight fires only near edges (suggesting the SwiftUI `.onDrop` hit-test is being blocked by the terminal NSView underneath), or not at all. Lifting and re-entering sometimes triggers it.

## Fix hint

The `TerminalPlaceholderView` layer hosts the `.onDrop` modifier. The AppKit `AppTerminalView` (libghostty NSView) sits beneath it and likely consumes mouse events before SwiftUI's drop session can track cursor position over the placeholder. Potential approaches:

1. **Move `.onDrop` higher in the hierarchy** — apply `PaneSwapDropTarget` to the pane chrome layer (above the terminal host) rather than to `TerminalPlaceholderView`, so the hit-test region is never shadowed by the NSView.
2. **Use an overlay instead of a background** — add a transparent `Color.clear.onDrop(...)` overlay on top of the terminal view so it sits above the NSView in z-order and receives drag events first.
3. **Widen the drop target to `PaneView` root** — attach `.onDrop` to the outer `PaneView` container instead of the placeholder, which is always the full pane frame and is SwiftUI-native throughout.

## Root cause

`PaneSwapDropTarget` applied `.onDrop` directly to its `content` (the ZStack of `TerminalPlaceholderView`s). The AppKit terminal NSView, positioned by `TerminalHostStore`, sits above SwiftUI content in the AppKit z-order. It intercepts drag events before SwiftUI's hit-test can evaluate the `.onDrop` modifier, so the highlight only fired near pane edges (the narrow strip of SwiftUI-rendered chrome) where the NSView didn't cover.

## Fix

Moved the `.onDrop` handler from the `content` surface onto a `Color.clear.contentShape(Rectangle())` SwiftUI overlay inside `PaneSwapDropTarget`. SwiftUI overlays are evaluated in SwiftUI's own rendering pass; attaching `.onDrop` there puts the drop hit-test above the AppKit NSView in z-order, so drag events are seen reliably over the entire pane body.

The accent-color stroke highlight overlay is retained as a second `.overlay` after the drop overlay, with `.allowsHitTesting(false)` so it doesn't interfere with input.

## Files changed

- `BattyKit/Sources/BattyKit/Views/PaneView.swift` — restructured `PaneSwapDropTarget.body` to apply `.onDrop` to a transparent `Color.clear` overlay rather than to `content` directly, ensuring the drop hit-test occurs above the AppKit terminal NSView.

## Gotchas

`Color.clear` by itself has no hit area in SwiftUI — `.contentShape(Rectangle())` is required to give the transparent overlay a hittable frame. Without it, the overlay would not participate in SwiftUI's hit-test and the drop would still not fire.

The terminal NSView is placed by `TerminalHostStore` in AppKit directly, not in the SwiftUI tree, so any `.onDrop` applied to the `content` of a modifier is below the NSView in AppKit z-order regardless of SwiftUI's rendering hierarchy. SwiftUI overlays, being rendered in a separate SwiftUI pass on top of the NSViewRepresentable host, avoid this problem.

**Post-ship regression (click blocking):** The initial resolution omitted `.allowsHitTesting(false)` on the `Color.clear` overlay. `Color.clear` with `.contentShape(Rectangle())` absorbs all pointer events including regular clicks, making the terminal unreachable by mouse. Fixed in `bf84304` by adding `.allowsHitTesting(false)` after `.onDrop`. AppKit's drag-and-drop routing (via `NSDraggingDestination`) operates independently of SwiftUI's gesture hit-test, so `.onDrop` continues to fire even with hit-testing disabled.
