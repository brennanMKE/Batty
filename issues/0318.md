## Description

Batty redistributes ~485 terminal color themes it did not author, and ships no copy of their license notice anywhere — in the app or in the repo. The licensing itself is fine (MIT throughout the chain), but **MIT's one condition is that the copyright notice and permission notice be included in all copies or substantial portions of the software**. Redistributing the theme data in the app bundle is squarely that, and the obligation is currently unmet.

**Provenance chain, verified:**

- `docs/themes.md` §2 ("Theme catalog source") documents that `GhosttyThemeCatalog.allThemes` — "~485 entries, generated from `mbadolato/iTerm2-Color-Schemes`" — ships inside the upstream `Lakr233/libghostty-spm` package, and that Batty merges it with its own small original set via `BattyThemeCatalog` (`BattyKit/Sources/BattyKit/Theme/BattyThemeCatalog.swift`). So the chain is `mbadolato/iTerm2-Color-Schemes` → `Lakr233/libghostty-spm` → Batty's merged catalog.
- `libghostty-spm`'s `LICENSE` is MIT, "Copyright (c) 2026 @Lakr233" (verified against the local clone at `~/Developer/brennanMKE/libghostty-spm`), with the standard "The above copyright notice and this permission notice shall be included in all copies or substantial portions" clause. `iTerm2-Color-Schemes` is documented as MIT as well — verify its exact notice text during implementation rather than trusting this line.

**What exists today, re-verified 2026-08-07:** a search of `BattyKit/Sources`, `Batty/`, `docs/`, `README.md`, and `website/` found no acknowledgements pane, no third-party-licenses file, and no license-notice reproduction anywhere. The one thing that *does* exist is a single credits line in the About panel (`BattyKit/Sources/BattyKit/Views/AboutPanel.swift:16-18`): "Built on libghostty and SlidingTabs." That is a name mention, not a license notice — it reproduces no copyright holder and no permission notice, and it doesn't mention the themes, `iTerm2-Color-Schemes`, or any other dependency. The repo's root `LICENSE` is Batty's own MIT notice only.

**Framing — record this so the issue is not misread as a #0310 follow-up:** this came out of the IP review around Batty's 22 original pop-culture themes (#0310). That review concluded the original themes' *names* carry modest, well-precedented trademark exposure — but that **this attribution gap is the one concrete, currently-unmet legal obligation**, and it concerns the ~485 *upstream* themes Batty redistributes, not Batty's own.

## Resolution notes

> 🟢 Resolved 2026-08-08 — `THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.md` at the repo root, with a byte-identical in-app copy shipped as a Help topic, reproduces the notice for every third-party component that ships in the bundle.

The obligation this issue was filed about — the ~485 upstream themes — is discharged, and review widened the inventory well past it. The scope grew twice: round 1 found three components shipping as **resources** rather than as `Package.resolved` pins (Prism.js, `bash-preexec.sh`, and the math fonts), and round 2 found that three shipped files are **GPLv3**, not the MIT the document asserted. One carve-out is filed as **#0323**.

## Root cause

Attribution was never built. The only credit anywhere was `AboutPanel.swift`'s "Built on libghostty and SlidingTabs." — a name mention, reproducing no copyright holder and no permission notice, and not mentioning the themes at all. The repo's root `LICENSE` is Batty's own MIT notice only.

The reason the gap was this wide: dependencies were reasoned about as *packages*, and several third parties ship as *resources inside* packages, where no manifest names them. Prism.js is bundled into `textual` by a script that strips its copyright header. `bash-preexec.sh` is vendored inside Ghostty's shell integration. The math fonts are inside `swiftui-math`'s resource bundle. None appear in `Package.resolved`.

## Fix

- **`THIRD-PARTY-LICENSES.md`** at the repo root — the full inventory, each component's license text reproduced verbatim from a primary source: iTerm2-Color-Schemes (the themes), libghostty-spm, the Ghostty xcframework binary, the Ghostty runtime resources, bash-preexec, MSDisplayLink, Sparkle (plus its four bundled external notices), textual, Prism.js, swiftui-math and its bundled fonts, swift-concurrency-extras, and swift-argument-parser. `SlidingTabs` is excluded as first-party; `swift-snapshot-testing` as test-only.
- **In-app surface** — a byte-identical copy shipped as Help topic `09-third-party-licenses.md`, reachable at Help ▸ Batty Help ▸ Third-Party Licenses, reusing the seam the other eight topics use. `AboutPanel.swift`'s credits point there in plain text.
- **`Resources/ghostty-runtime/ATTRIBUTION.md`** — rewritten from a blanket MIT claim to "mixed", naming the three GPLv3 files and bash-preexec, and cross-linked to the inventory.
- **`ThirdPartyLicensesSyncTests`** — asserts the repo-root file and the shipped copy stay byte-identical, so the two cannot drift silently.

The in-app surface deliberately avoids a clickable `file://` link: macOS has no default handler for `.md` on a stock install, so the link would have opened a "no application set to open the document" chooser rather than the notices.

## Review

Reviewer: `claude-opus-5`, three rounds, a fresh agent each round. Every round found something real.

**Round 1** machine-diffed all ten license blocks against primary sources and found them verbatim — including Sparkle's four bundled external notices, which it called out as the easiest thing to get subtly wrong. Then it found three components shipping as resources that the pin-scoped inventory missed entirely: **Prism.js** (128 KB in `textual_Textual.bundle`, copyright header stripped by textual's bundler, MIT © 2012 Lea Verou), **`bash-preexec.sh`** (MIT © Ryan Caloras, shipped with no license text at all), and **13 math fonts** under STIX and GUST/LPPL filed as plain MIT.

**Round 2** verified those four new blocks verbatim, then found the blocker: three files Batty ships under `Contents/Resources/ghostty/shell-integration/` are **GPLv3**, and the document affirmatively stated MIT for that whole path. The word "GPL" appeared nowhere in 1231 lines. It confirmed via `gh api` that the headers are upstream Ghostty's own rather than local drift, and noted the round-2 `ATTRIBUTION.md` edit had actively reinforced the error by framing bash-preexec as the sole exception.

**Round 3** verified the GPLv3 notice is an exact string match against all three shipped headers, checked the canonical license URL resolves and really is 674 lines, re-ran the sweep independently over whole file bodies, and confirmed no other shipping resource tree was missed — checking every `cp`/`rsync`/`ditto` in `scripts/`, all three `PBXShellScriptBuildPhase` entries, and every checkout's `Package.swift` for resource declarations. It ruled the Kovid Goyal copyright attribution should **stay**: GPLv3 §4 asks for an appropriate copyright notice, so naming no rightsholder would be less compliant, not safer.

## Gotchas

- **Batty ships three GPLv3 files.** Ghostty's Kitty-derived `bash/ghostty.bash`, `zsh/.zshenv`, and `zsh/ghostty-integration`. They ship unmodified in source form, so GPLv3 §4 (verbatim copying) is the operative clause rather than §§5–6, and nothing about Batty's own MIT licensing is affected. But this was invisible until this issue, and anyone reasoning about Batty's license surface needs to know.
- **Package manifests do not tell you what ships.** Three of the components here are resources vendored *inside* other packages, named in no manifest — and one of them has its copyright header deliberately stripped by the bundling script that produces it. Auditing `Package.resolved` is not sufficient; the built `.app` is the source of truth.
- **Two copies of a legal document will drift** unless something enforces it. That is what `ThirdPartyLicensesSyncTests` is for; do not "fix" a failure there by editing only one file.
- **Per-theme authorship inside iTerm2-Color-Schemes is not cataloged**, and 10 of the 12 math fonts' individual licenses are marked unverified. Both are deliberate, documented gaps rather than guesses: upstream publishes no per-item manifest in either case, and the governing license files ship alongside the assets. An admitted gap beats a fabricated notice.
- **`scripts/bundle-ghostty-resources.sh` does not copy `Resources/ghostty-runtime/` wholesale** — it copies the single terminfo file and rsyncs only `ghostty/shell-integration/`. A file added at the `ghostty-runtime/` root would not ship. This matters for #0323.

## Expected behavior

Every third-party component Batty redistributes has its copyright notice and license text reproduced in a surface a user can find, and the repo carries the same inventory.

## Actual behavior

No attribution surface exists; the only credit anywhere is the About panel's unversioned "Built on libghostty and SlidingTabs." one-liner.
